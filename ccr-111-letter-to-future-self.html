Letter to Your Future Self

Name the outcomes you want out of senior year, work each one backwards into checkpoints you can actually hit, and seal it inside a letter you open in May.

90-minute block Grade 12 6 sections Turn in digitally

Why this letter 8 min

Read this part before you type anything.

Senior year is not one year

It is four. The fall where everything is due at once. The winter where you are waiting to hear back. The spring where half your class checks out. And the last three weeks where all of it lands at the same time.

The version of you sitting here in August has something the May version will not have: you can still see the whole year at once. You know what you actually want out of it. By April that gets buried under deadlines, decisions, and other people's opinions about your life.

So write it down now, while it is clear. This letter is not a diary entry. It is evidence — a record of what you said mattered, before the year had a chance to talk you out of it.

What you are building today. Three outcomes you want by June, each one worked backwards into November, January, and April checkpoints, wrapped in a letter to yourself. We open the checkpoints at every marking period. You get the letter back in May.

The checker is a mirror, not a grade. It can see whether an outcome is countable, deadlined, and provable. It cannot see whether it matters to you. That part is the "why this one" box, and that is the part that decides whether you are still doing this in February.

Backwards planner 16 min

An outcome without a plan is a wish with better grammar. Start at June and walk backwards: where does this have to be in April, in January, in November?

Worked example. JUNE: Statistics finished at 85+  ←  APRIL: 85+ average going into the last marking period  ←  JANUARY: no missing assignments, B on the midterm  ←  NOVEMBER: tutoring every Tuesday, first quarter above 80.
